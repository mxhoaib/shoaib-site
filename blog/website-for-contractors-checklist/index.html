Website Checklist for Contractors and Stone Companies

For contractors, fit-out companies and marble suppliers, a website is often the first impression a potential client gets. A slow, outdated or unclear site can lose work before you even get a phone call. This checklist covers what a modern construction or stone business website needs, based on established web guidance rather than trends.

1. A clear message above the fold

Within a few seconds, visitors should know what you do, where you work, and how to contact you. A strong headline, a short supporting line, and one obvious call to action are more effective than a long slideshow.

2. Real project photos

Nothing builds trust like real work. Use your own photos of completed projects, ideally with a short description of the scope, materials and location. Avoid generic stock images of construction sites; clients can tell. If you don't have good photos yet, clean diagrams or drawings of your capabilities are better than stock.

3. Service pages, not just a list

Create a separate page for each main service, such as kitchen countertops, stone cladding, joinery or fit-out. Each page should explain the service, the process, the materials, and the areas you cover. Separate pages help visitors find what they need and help search engines understand your expertise.

4. Fast loading on mobile

Most visitors arrive on a phone. Google's Core Web Vitals measure loading speed (LCP), responsiveness (INP) and visual stability (CLS). Practical steps include compressing images, using modern formats like WebP, setting image dimensions, and avoiding heavy scripts and sliders.

5. Contact details that are easy to find

Put your contact method in the header or footer of every page. Keep your business name, address and contact details consistent across your website, Google Business Profile and directories. This consistency, often called NAP consistency, supports local search visibility.

6. Local SEO basics

If you serve a specific city or region:

  • Set up and verify a Google Business Profile.
  • Mention the areas you serve naturally on service pages.
  • Add LocalBusiness or ProfessionalService structured data.
  • Encourage satisfied clients to leave genuine reviews. Never buy or fake reviews; it breaches platform policies and can harm your business.

7. Technical SEO essentials

  • A unique title and meta description for each page.
  • One H1 heading per page with a logical heading structure.
  • An XML sitemap submitted to Google Search Console.
  • A robots.txt file that doesn't block important pages.
  • HTTPS across the whole site.
  • Descriptive alt text on images.

Google's own SEO Starter Guide is an excellent free reference.

8. Helpful content

A blog or resources section lets you answer the questions clients ask: how long does a countertop take, what is the difference between granite and quartz, what is included in a fit-out quote. Helpful, original content builds trust and can attract search traffic over time. Write for people first, not for search engines.

9. Proof of expertise

Explain who you are, how long you have been working, and what qualifications or memberships you hold. Only include facts you can stand behind. Vague claims are less convincing than specific, honest details.

10. A simple enquiry process

Long forms put people off. Ask for the essentials: name, email, project type, location and a short description. Make it easy to attach drawings or photos.

11. Security and maintenance

Keep software updated, use HTTPS, and back up your site. Static websites (plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript) are fast, cheap to host and have a small attack surface, which makes them a good choice for many small businesses.

12. Measure and improve

Install Google Analytics 4 and Google Search Console to see how people find and use your site. Review which pages attract enquiries and improve the ones that don't.
