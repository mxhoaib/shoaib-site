On almost every construction project someone asks, "Don't we already have drawings?" The answer is that different drawings do different jobs. Design and construction drawings describe what should be built. Shop drawings describe exactly how a particular trade will make and install it. This guide answers the questions people most often search for about shop drawings and how they compare with other drawing types.

What is a shop drawing in construction?

A shop drawing is a detailed drawing prepared by a contractor, subcontractor, fabricator or supplier to show how a specific element will be manufactured and installed. It translates the design intent into exact sizes, materials, joints, fixings and sequences that a workshop can follow. Common examples include stone cladding and flooring, joinery, steelwork, glazing, ductwork and rebar.

What are construction drawings?

Construction drawings, sometimes called the issued-for-construction (IFC) set, are prepared by the architect and engineers. They show the overall building: plans, elevations, sections, structural and MEP layouts, plus general details and specifications. They describe what is required and where, but they deliberately leave many fabrication decisions to the trade that will build each part.

Shop drawings vs construction drawings: what is the difference?

  • Author: construction drawings come from the design team; shop drawings come from the contractor or fabricator.
  • Purpose: construction drawings define design intent; shop drawings define how that intent will be made.
  • Detail level: shop drawings go further, for example individual piece sizes, joint widths, fixing positions and edge profiles.
  • Approval: shop drawings are submitted to the consultant or architect for review before fabrication starts.

Shop drawings vs fabrication drawings: are they the same?

They overlap. Many firms use the terms interchangeably. Where a distinction is made, the shop drawing is the submittal that shows layout and details for approval, and the fabrication or piece drawing is the workshop document that shows each part on its own with every cut dimension. In stone work, for example, the approved layout leads to individual piece drawings and a cutting list.

Shop drawings vs submittals: what is the difference?

A submittal is the package a contractor sends for approval. Shop drawings are one type of submittal. Others include product data sheets, material samples, mock-ups and test certificates. A stone submittal might combine shop drawings, a sample of the marble, technical data and the fixing system details.

How do shop drawings differ from as-built drawings?

Shop drawings are prepared before fabrication to show what is going to be made. As-built drawings are prepared after installation to record what was actually built, including any changes made on site. Approved shop drawings often form the starting point for as-builts.

Who prepares and approves shop drawings?

The trade contractor or fabricator prepares them, often through a specialist drafter. The main contractor checks them for coordination with other trades, and the architect or consultant reviews them against the design. Review stamps vary, such as approved, approved with comments or revise and resubmit. What should a good shop drawing include?

  • a title block with project, drawing number, revision and scale;
  • references to the relevant construction drawings;
  • plans, elevations and sections at a workable scale;
  • dimensions sufficient to fabricate without guessing;
  • material, finish and thickness callouts;
  • joint, fixing and interface details with adjacent trades;
  • a revision table with changes clouded.

Why do shop drawings matter so much?

Because construction drawings rarely contain enough information to manufacture from. Shop drawings expose clashes early, confirm site dimensions and give the design team a chance to accept details before money is spent on materials. A mistake caught on paper costs very little; the same mistake found after stone is cut costs a slab.

What is the difference between shop drawings and working drawings?

"Working drawings" is an older, more general term for the drawings used to build from. In some regions it means the architect's detailed construction set; in others it is used loosely for any drawing on site. Shop drawings are narrower: they belong to a single trade and are submitted for review. If a client asks for working drawings, clarify whether they mean the design team's construction set or a trade's shop drawings, because the scope and the author are completely different.

How long does a shop drawing review usually take?

Review periods are normally set in the contract or the project's submittal procedure, and they vary by client and consultant. Late or incomplete submissions are one of the most common causes of programme delay, so plan for at least one round of comments. The fastest approvals come from drawings that reference the construction set clearly, answer the consultant's likely questions up front, and cloud every change on resubmission.

What are the most common shop drawing mistakes?

  • dimensions taken from the design drawings without checking site measurements;
  • missing interfaces with other trades, such as stone meeting glazing or MEP fixtures;
  • unclear material callouts or finishes;
  • revisions issued without clouds or an updated revision table;
  • drawings at a scale too small to read fixing details.

Can shop drawings be outsourced?

Yes, and many fabricators do this when their in-house team is busy. A remote drafter needs the construction drawings, site measurements, the material and the fabricator's preferred details.
